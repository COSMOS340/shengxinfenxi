## Reason the formal object is rejected

The source article retained 155,397 high-quality cells. The PC object retained 238,934 cells after its primary QC and scDblFinder filtering. A different total is not automatically an error, but the cell-level QC evidence confirms that this difference is methodological rather than a benign software-version effect.

- Nine clusters contain at least five mitochondrial genes in their Top10 markers.
- These nine clusters contain 53,708 cells, or 22.478% of the retained object.
- Median mitochondrial fractions reach 92.06%, 96.76%, and 99.82% in clusters 2, 12, and 27.
- Additional clusters with lineage markers in their Top10 still have high median mitochondrial fractions, so the nine-cluster flag is not exhaustive.
- The PC method derived per-sample mitochondrial thresholds, and several thresholds approach 100%. This allows a sample dominated by damaged cells to define its own high baseline.

The source article used a different procedure:

1. Retain cells with 500 to 5,000 detected genes and 400 to 25,000 UMIs; these bounds were also used to remove most empty partitions and doublets.
2. Normalize and integrate with Seurat RPCA, use 2,000 variable genes, regress UMI counts, and run the first clustering with 20 PCs at resolution 1.2.
3. Assign six broad compartments from explicit marker sets.
4. Remove epithelial cells with mitochondrial proportion above 75%.
5. Fit separate median-centered, MAD-variance normal distributions for combined lymphoid cells, myeloid cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells, then remove one-sided high mitochondrial outliers at Bonferroni-adjusted `p < 0.05`.
6. Rebuild the final 155,397-cell atlas after this compartment-aware QC.

The paper does not report an explicit computational doublet caller. Therefore, scDblFinder exclusion cannot be described as replication of the source method. It will be retained as a secondary sensitivity analysis, while the formal object follows the paper's gene and UMI bounds.
